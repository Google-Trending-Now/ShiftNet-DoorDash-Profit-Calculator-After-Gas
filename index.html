How This DoorDash Profit Calculator After Gas Works

Most gig apps flash a big gross number after a four-hour dinner rush, then leave you to figure out what the fuel pump and odometer actually took back. This gig worker mileage profit tool uses four straightforward formulas so you see the exact cash left in your pocket.

1. Gas Cost

Gas Cost = (Miles ÷ MPG) × Gas Price

First we calculate how many gallons your car burned on the shift, then multiply by your local pump price. Use your car's real city/stop-and-go MPG—not the highway sticker number.

2. Maintenance & Depreciation

Wear Cost = Miles × Wear Rate ($/mi)

Every 100 delivery miles chips away at your tires, brake pads, oil life, and resale value. Defaulting to $0.12/mile gives a grounded cushion, and you can adjust or toggle it off anytime.

3. Net Profit & True Net Hourly

Net Hourly = (Gross − Gas − Wear) ÷ Hours

Count your full dash time—including waiting in drive-thrus and sitting in parking lots between pings—not just active delivery minutes.

4. Estimated IRS Mileage Deduction

Tax Write-Off = Business Miles × $0.70

We keep your IRS deduction separate from out-of-pocket expenses. It reduces your taxable self-employment income on Schedule C rather than coming straight out of tonight's cash.

3 Practical Rules to Keep More of Your Pay

Running an honest delivery driver expense calculator is step one. Changing which offers you accept is how you actually raise your hourly rate.

  1. Rule 01

    Aim for $1.50 to $2.00+ Per Mile

    Declining a $3.25 no-tip order going 5 miles is the fastest raise you can give yourself. Between waiting in a slow drive-thru and driving back, low-ball offers often net less than minimum wage once gas comes out.

  2. Rule 02

    Price In Your Deadhead Miles

    An $11 offer going 6 miles looks solid until it drops you in a rural neighborhood with zero restaurants. If you have to drive 5 empty miles back to your hotspot, that's really an 11-mile trip paying $1.00/mile gross.

  3. Rule 03

    Track Every Mile From the Driveway

    Don't rely only on the active delivery mileage summary apps email in January—those usually skip miles driven between offers. Log your odometer from start to finish so you don't leave hundreds of Schedule C tax dollars behind.

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers on using this gig worker mileage profit tool for food delivery and rideshare shifts.

Can I use this as an Uber driver net earnings calculator too?

Yes. The math is identical whether you drive for DoorDash, Uber, Uber Eats, Lyft, Instacart, or Grubhub. Enter your total payout including tips and surges, your full odometer miles for the shift, and your car's real-world MPG to see what you actually kept.

Why isn't the IRS mileage deduction subtracted from my Net Take-Home Profit?

Because the IRS standard mileage rate ($0.70 per mile by default) isn't an extra cash bill on top of gas and repairs. It's a tax write-off that lowers your taxable self-employment income on Schedule C. Subtracting both gas and $0.70/mile would double-count your car expenses.

How much should I enter for maintenance and wear-and-tear per mile?

Most drivers start at $0.10 to $0.15 per mile for oil changes, tires, brakes, fluids, and gradual depreciation. If you drive an older paid-off sedan with cheap parts, $0.10/mi may fit; if you drive a newer SUV or truck, $0.18 to $0.25/mi is closer to reality. You can toggle this delivery driver expense calculator line on or off anytime.

Should I include deadhead miles in this gig worker mileage profit tool?

Always. Include every mile from the moment you go online and pull out of your driveway until you park back at home, including driving back from a drop-off to a busy zone. Leaving out empty return miles makes a shift look much more profitable on paper than it is at the pump.

Is my saved shift log private or sent to a server?

Everything stays on your phone or computer. Your shift log is saved strictly inside your browser's localStorage with zero account sign-up and zero external tracking. Clearing your browser data or tapping Clear Log erases it completely.
